A LITTLE SCIENCE. A LOT OF CURIOSITY.

Follow the clues.

A thoughtful introduction to image classification — with a cat’s-eye view of the natural world.

01 / OBSERVE

A clear photograph

Choose one cat, large in the frame, with its face and coat visible. Natural photographs work best; tiny subjects, illustrations, and mixed groups can confuse the models.

02 / COMPARE

Two sets of eyes

A general vision model first compares cats with other animals and objects. BioCLIP 2.5 then compares the photograph with descriptions of the 41 species in our catalogue.

03 / DISCOVER

A starting point

Explore the closest matches and the clues that separate them. If the evidence is weak or species look alike, the result stays uncertain. Sometimes, no reliable cat match is found.

HONEST BY DESIGN

Similarity isn’t certainty.

The scores describe how closely an image and a species description align in the model. They are not percentages, probabilities, or proof of identity. Rejection rules are conservative heuristics, not a guarantee that every unfamiliar image will be rejected.

This is an educational demonstration. It does not identify breeds, confirm hybrids, detect every animal in a group, or replace an expert’s assessment. Geography and other evidence may be essential for a reliable species identification.

A note on names

Our 41 species concepts follow the IUCN Cat Classification Task Force’s 2017 reference, including the domestic cat. Taxonomy evolves: some more recent splits are grouped under the older concepts here. This is a versioned catalogue, not a claim to cover every currently proposed species.
